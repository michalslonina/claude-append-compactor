# Handoff-template findings (experiment branch)

Whether the plugin's context injection should add a structured handoff template, and
if so how large it should be. Everything here is from a synthetic benchmark; nothing
was merged, and the branch exists to hold the evidence for that decision.

Artifacts are outside the repo: results and summaries live in
`/tmp/ac-template-experiment/bench/`. This file is the summary; the JSON is the record.

## What ran

| run | arms | scenarios | depths | model | controls |
|---|---|---|--:|---|---|
| v8 | 7 arms incl. `shuffled_template` | 4 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 55/55 |
| v9 | `full`, `current_goals`, `current_goals_template`, `template_minimal` | 6 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 77/77 |
| v10 | 11 arms: every section dropped once, plus anchors and a deterministic extractor | 6 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 77/77 |
| v11 | `current_goals`, `template_minimal`, `template_det` | 6 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 89/89 |
| v3 | template arms | 6 | 24/60/96 | deepseek-flash | 50/50 |

`n` per arm is the row count — one deterministic call per (scenario, depth, arm)
cell. v9 is 12 rows per arm, not 12 samples per cell.

**v9 ran with `TC_BENCH_COVERAGE_MIN=-1`**, which accepts templates that never
mention the early decision or the later correction. That is not a detail to skim:
it means every v9 template is known to be missing the two fields the strict rule
exists to require. The results file predates the `settings.template_coverage_min`
field that now records this, so the policy is stated here instead.

Controls passed 77/77, including the known-negative no-context arm at 0.33 against
a chance of 0.25.

## Result 1 — the template is worth adding, and most of it is not

| arm | extra chars vs `current_goals` | extra tok/turn | constr | correction | values | fact | open-loop | choice | task |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `current_goals` (today) | 0 | 0 | 0.67 | 0.78 | 0.56 | 0.60 | 0.21 | 0.83 | 0.67 |
| `current_goals_template` | +1999 | +500 | 0.75 | 0.72 | 0.88 | 0.79 | 0.67 | 0.92 | 0.92 |
| `template_minimal` | **+537** | **+134** | 0.71 | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.75 |
| `full` (whole transcript) | +2232 | +558 | 0.73 | 0.92 | 0.88 | 0.69 | 0.62 | 1.00 | 0.92 |

Paired against the full template (`--baseline current_goals_template`, 12 paired
cells):

| metric | minimal − full | wins | losses | ties |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| open-loop | **+0.333** | 4 | 0 | 8 |
| correction | **+0.194** | 5 | 0 | 7 |
| values | +0.021 | 2 | 1 | 9 |
| decision | −0.014 | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| constraint | −0.042 | 0 | 2 | 10 |
| fact | −0.045 | 3 | 3 | 6 |
| error | −0.111 | 1 | 3 | 8 |
| **task success** | **−0.167** | 1 | 3 | 8 |

Cutting the template from ten sections to five costs **73% fewer added tokens**
(134 vs 500 per turn). Minimal is strictly better on three metrics (open-loop,
correction, values), exactly equal on four (goal, success, artifact, choice), and
within ties on decision, fact and next-step. The real losses are task success
(−0.167, 1 win against 3 losses), error recall (−0.111) and constraint recall
(2 losses, no wins).

The open-loop gain is where it matters most. At depth 96, `current_goals` delivers
0.08 open-loop recall, the full template 0.50, the minimal template 1.00 — the
pending ask survives the distraction tail only when it is short enough to compete.

## Result 2 — the binding constraint is the generator, not the template

Every template in v9 was generated by qwen2.5:7b, and **all 30 of them** (6
scenarios, anchored and regenerated, both depths) have empty decision and
correction fields — coverage `decision=0.0, correction=0.0`, while constraints
sit at 0.75. A strict probe that makes those fields blocking rejected **9 of 9**
attempts across three scenarios, every one for the same reason.

So the template approach on a small local model does not carry decisions at all —
the whole-transcript arm reaches 0.97 decision recall against 0.64 for the
templates. That gap is a generation limit, not a template-design limit, and no
amount of section reshuffling closes it.

This also explains the v8 result that replacement arms lose corrections (−0.38):
a template that omits decisions cannot stand in for a ledger that still has them.

## Result 3 — the five sections are not equally load-bearing (v10)

v9 said the minimal template beats the full one for a quarter of the tokens, but
not *which* of its five sections did the work. v10 drops each one in turn and
measures the paired difference against `template_minimal` over the same 12
cells. A section that earns its tokens loses recall when removed; a section that
is dead weight does not.

| variant | extra chars | what changes vs `template_minimal` |
|---|--:|---|
| `minus_open` | +413 | **open-loop −0.833** (0 wins / 11 losses, 1.00 → 0.17) |
| `minus_values` | +374 | **values −0.375** (0 W / 10 L), **fact −0.174** (1 W / 9 L) |
| `minus_files` | +439 | nothing: next-step +0.056 (1 W / 0 L), correction −0.028 |
| `minus_errors` | +508 | nothing: error −0.028 (0 W / 1 L), decision +0.028 |
| `plus_success` | +607 | nothing measurable, **including task success +0.000 (0 W / 0 L / 12 ties)** |
| `anchors` | +613 | decision +0.028, next-step +0.056 (1 W / 0 L), choice +0.083, task +0.083, error −0.056 (2 W / 3 L) |

Two sections carry nearly all the value. **Open questions / pending asks** is
the whole of the open-loop result: remove it and the open-loop number collapses
to 0.17, which is also where `current_goals` sits. **Critical exact values**
costs two metrics when removed (values and fact, which is their mean). Those
two are worth their ~40 tokens each.

**Files and errors are dead weight for this generator, not in principle.** The
7B leaves both sections nearly empty (Result 2), so removing them removes
nothing and the ablation cannot price them. That is a limit of the evidence, and
it is why the deterministic arm exists.

> **Correction (added after v11).** This paragraph was wrong, and the ablation
> behind it is void. `_is_empty` treated a dict as empty when *none* of its
> children was empty — inverted. `_as_list` filters every list item through it,
> so any section whose value was a dict (`files_symbols_references`,
> `errors_and_fixes`, `progress`, `decisions_and_rationale`) rendered **nothing**
> in v8, v9 and v10. The `minus_files` and `minus_errors` arms were therefore
> removing sections that were already blank in every arm, which is why they
> measured exactly zero. The same bug capped `template_minimal` at 535 rendered
> chars when the identical cached template renders 804 once fixed — so the v9
> cost figure understates the template it claimed to price. The fix landed before
> v11, which is why v11's `template_minimal` is larger and scores differently.
> Treat every files/errors number in v8–v10 as unmeasured, not as zero.

**The task-success deficit is not a missing success-criteria section.** v9
measured `template_minimal` at −0.167 on task success against the full template,
and the obvious suspect was the dropped success criteria. Adding a success
section back changes task success by exactly 0.000 across all 12 cells. Whatever
costs the compact arms their task success, it is not that section — most likely
it is the loss of the long-tail context the full template keeps, but v10 does
not isolate it.

**Anchors are free.** Tagging each bullet with the turn it came from costs 76
characters and never loses: choice and task success +0.083, next-step +0.056,
with one small error-recall loss that ties out.

## Result 4 — copied facts beat generated ones on decisions, lose on recall (v11)

v11 pits the repaired deterministic extractor against the model-generated
minimal template, with the rendering bug fixed and the same cache, seed and
depths. Three arms, 36 rows, 89/89 controls.

| arm | extra tok/turn | constr | correction | error | values | fact | open-loop | choice | task |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `current_goals` (today) | 0 | 0.67 | 0.78 | 0.58 | 0.58 | 0.58 | 0.29 | 0.83 | 0.67 |
| `template_minimal` | +201 | 0.71 | 0.83 | 1.00 | 0.96 | 0.98 | 1.00 | 0.83 | 0.67 |
| `template_det` | **+313** | 0.73 | 0.86 | 0.94 | 0.79 | 0.87 | 0.71 | **1.00** | **0.92** |

Both arms beat today's injection comfortably. Against `current_goals`,
`template_minimal` gains open-loop **+0.708** (10 wins / 0 losses), values
+0.375, fact +0.396, error +0.417; `template_det` gains error +0.361, open-loop
+0.417, fact +0.285, values +0.208, and is the only arm that moves task success
(+0.250, 3 W / 0 L) and decision choice (+0.167, 2 W / 0 L).

Head to head, the deterministic arm is **not** the free win its offline ceiling
suggested. It loses open-loop −0.292 (0 W / 5 L), values −0.167 (0 W / 6 L),
fact −0.111 (0 W / 7 L) and error −0.056, while winning task success +0.250
(3 W / 0 L) and choice +0.167 (2 W / 0 L) — for 56% more tokens.

The offline measurement said the deterministic template *carries* open-loop,
values and errors at 1.00; measured through the model it delivers 0.71, 0.79 and
0.94. So the gap is in delivery, not extraction: a longer, denser template that
mixes verbatim facts with tool-output noise is harder for a 7B to use than a
short one written in its own register. Copying the fact is not the same as
making it usable.

## Result 5 — model-emitted tags can be made verifiable (journal probe)

A different injection mechanism from the templates above: the model drops tagged
lines as it works (`※v` value, `※e` error, `※f` fix, `※t` task, `※d` done), and
a regex harvests them into the handoff. Marker is `※` (U+203B) at line start;
the entry runs to end of line, because every punctuation candidate occurs inside
the values we care about (`2.7.1`, `src/export/csv_writer.py`,
`Hit OSError: [Errno 24]`).

DeepSeek, synthetic situations, 21 calls across three experiments.

**Emission.** With the grammar explained, 21/21 tags were well-formed across
three runs; with no instruction, 0 tags in all three controls. `※d` closures
appeared in 3/3 instructed runs. Syntax is not the risk.

**Invention is the risk.** In the first experiment 6 of 21 tags were invented —
including a proposed constant tagged as an established fact
(`※v MERGE_STALL_TIMEOUT_S 30.0`) and a model inference tagged as an observed
error. A regex cannot tell a copy from a plausible invention.

**A contiguous-copy check fixes it, and the model can be steered to pass it.**
Grounding = the body appears contiguously in the supplied context (case,
whitespace and edge punctuation normalised; word overlap deliberately does not
count). Verifier controls 5/5, including the actual invented constant and a
stitched-words negative.

| condition | v/e emitted | v/e grounded | cost vs baseline |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| A: tag as you work | 16 | 10 (62.5%) | — |
| B: verbatim-only | 11 | **11 (100%)** | −32% tags, format collapse in 1 of 5 |
| C: verbatim + source turn (`※v 12 …`) | 14 | **14 (100%)**, all anchors valid | −23% tags |

C dominates B: same verifiability, more volume, and the anchor gives an auditor
a place to check. Persistence held over 5 sequential turns with the protocol
stated once — 100% grounded on every turn, no format decay, though volume fell
from 10 tags to 2 and recovered to 6.

**What it costs.** The model tags only what it can copy, so derived reasoning is
dropped — a computed "1040 s used of 1200 s budget" is exactly the kind of
conclusion a handoff should carry and exactly what the verifier rejects. And
verifiable is not the same as useful: several `t`/`d` pairs merely echoed a user
instruction and closed it immediately.

So a verifiable journal holds **copied facts and open/closed state, not
reasoning** — which is the same division of labour v11 arrived at from the other
direction. Limits: n=5 situations per condition, one model, one sample per cell,
no repeats.

## Recommendation

1. **Prefer the additive, minimal template.** Template sections pay for themselves
   as an addition to the ledger; replacing the ledger with a template loses what
   the generator never wrote. (Cost figures from v9/v10 are understated — see the
   correction in Result 3. v11's numbers are the ones fixed after that bug.)
2. **Ship the generated minimal template as the default.** In v11's head-to-head
   it delivers the best recall per token: +201 tok/turn, open-loop 1.00, values
   0.96, fact 0.98, all from `current_goals` baselines near 0.3–0.6.
3. **Keep the deterministic extractor as the fallback, not the replacement.**
   It needs no generator, so it is the only arm that still works on a model that
   writes empty fields — and it is the only arm that improved task success and
   decision choice. But it costs 56% more tokens and loses recall, so it is the
   right answer only where a generation call is unavailable or unreliable.
4. **Before shipping the deterministic arm, cut its noise.** Its 8 value slots
   fill with tool-output chatter (`pytest -q -> 214 passed…`, `coverage -> 87.4
   percent…`), which is the most likely cause of its delivery gap and is a cheap
   fix: drop clauses whose only role is reporting a command's output.
5. **Keep the strict validation rule as the default**, and treat a run under
   `TC_BENCH_COVERAGE_MIN=-1` as a loose-template run. The rule is what surfaced
   Result 2; loosening it by default would hide it again.
6. **Add the tag journal only as the bookkeeping layer — open/closed state.**
   Result 5 prices it: grounded emission works in the source-anchored form
   (`※v 12 …`, 14/14 grounded, anchors all valid, −23% tags) and holds up over
   five turns without reminders. But the copy verifier rejects derived
   reasoning, and the verifier is the whole point — drop it and a third of the
   tags are invented. So facts and errors come from the template or the
   deterministic extractor, and the journal contributes only what neither can
   know: which tasks are still open. Before building the harvester, audit
   whether the `t`/`d` tags carry more than the instruction they echo back; in
   the probe several did not, and that check has not been run.

## Threats to validity

- Synthetic sessions, one small local model, one sample per cell. Paired, not a
  significance test; the tie counts above show how thin several of the differences
  are.
- The generator and the measured model are the same 7B, so Results 1 and 2 are
  entangled: the minimal arm looks good partly because the sections it drops were
  empty anyway.
- The strong-model arm (deepseek-flash) saturated at 1.00 in v3, so it cannot
  resolve the minimal-vs-full question either. Doing that properly needs both a
  capable generator and a harder probe.
