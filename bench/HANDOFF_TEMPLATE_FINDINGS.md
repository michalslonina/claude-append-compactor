# Handoff-template findings (experiment branch)

Whether the plugin's context injection should add a structured handoff template, and
if so how large it should be. Everything here is from a synthetic benchmark; nothing
was merged, and the branch exists to hold the evidence for that decision.

Artifacts are outside the repo: results and summaries live in
`/tmp/ac-template-experiment/bench/`. This file is the summary; the JSON is the record.

## What ran

| run | arms | scenarios | depths | model | controls |
|---|---|---|--:|---|---|
| v8 | 7 arms incl. `shuffled_template` | 4 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 55/55 |
| v9 (this) | `full`, `current_goals`, `current_goals_template`, `template_minimal` | 6 | 24/96 | qwen2.5:7b | 77/77 |
| v3 | template arms | 6 | 24/60/96 | deepseek-flash | 50/50 |

`n` per arm is the row count — one deterministic call per (scenario, depth, arm)
cell. v9 is 12 rows per arm, not 12 samples per cell.

**v9 ran with `TC_BENCH_COVERAGE_MIN=-1`**, which accepts templates that never
mention the early decision or the later correction. That is not a detail to skim:
it means every v9 template is known to be missing the two fields the strict rule
exists to require. The results file predates the `settings.template_coverage_min`
field that now records this, so the policy is stated here instead.

Controls passed 77/77, including the known-negative no-context arm at 0.33 against
a chance of 0.25.

## Result 1 — the template is worth adding, and most of it is not

| arm | extra chars vs `current_goals` | extra tok/turn | constr | correction | values | fact | open-loop | choice | task |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `current_goals` (today) | 0 | 0 | 0.67 | 0.78 | 0.56 | 0.60 | 0.21 | 0.83 | 0.67 |
| `current_goals_template` | +1999 | +500 | 0.75 | 0.72 | 0.88 | 0.79 | 0.67 | 0.92 | 0.92 |
| `template_minimal` | **+537** | **+134** | 0.71 | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.75 |
| `full` (whole transcript) | +2232 | +558 | 0.73 | 0.92 | 0.88 | 0.69 | 0.62 | 1.00 | 0.92 |

Paired against the full template (`--baseline current_goals_template`, 12 paired
cells):

| metric | minimal − full | wins | losses | ties |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| open-loop | **+0.333** | 4 | 0 | 8 |
| correction | **+0.194** | 5 | 0 | 7 |
| values | +0.021 | 2 | 1 | 9 |
| decision | −0.014 | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| constraint | −0.042 | 0 | 2 | 10 |
| fact | −0.045 | 3 | 3 | 6 |
| error | −0.111 | 1 | 3 | 8 |
| **task success** | **−0.167** | 1 | 3 | 8 |

Cutting the template from ten sections to five costs **73% fewer added tokens**
(134 vs 500 per turn). Minimal is strictly better on three metrics (open-loop,
correction, values), exactly equal on four (goal, success, artifact, choice), and
within ties on decision, fact and next-step. The real losses are task success
(−0.167, 1 win against 3 losses), error recall (−0.111) and constraint recall
(2 losses, no wins).

The open-loop gain is where it matters most. At depth 96, `current_goals` delivers
0.08 open-loop recall, the full template 0.50, the minimal template 1.00 — the
pending ask survives the distraction tail only when it is short enough to compete.

## Result 2 — the binding constraint is the generator, not the template

Every template in v9 was generated by qwen2.5:7b, and **all 30 of them** (6
scenarios, anchored and regenerated, both depths) have empty decision and
correction fields — coverage `decision=0.0, correction=0.0`, while constraints
sit at 0.75. A strict probe that makes those fields blocking rejected **9 of 9**
attempts across three scenarios, every one for the same reason.

So the template approach on a small local model does not carry decisions at all —
the whole-transcript arm reaches 0.97 decision recall against 0.64 for the
templates. That gap is a generation limit, not a template-design limit, and no
amount of section reshuffling closes it.

This also explains the v8 result that replacement arms lose corrections (−0.38):
a template that omits decisions cannot stand in for a ledger that still has them.

## Recommendation

1. **Prefer the additive, minimal template.** Template sections pay for themselves
   as an addition to the ledger, at ~134 tokens per turn; replacing the ledger with
   a template loses what the generator never wrote.
2. **Ship the five-section template, not the ten-section one**, unless task success
   is the metric that matters — then the full template's extra sections earn their
   ~370 additional tokens per turn. That is the one open trade-off this run cannot
   settle.
3. **Keep the strict validation rule as the default**, and treat a run under
   `TC_BENCH_COVERAGE_MIN=-1` as a loose-template run. The rule is what surfaced
   Result 2; loosening it by default would hide it again.
4. **Improve generation before shrinking the template further.** On qwen2.5:7b the
   template is already mostly empty, so the remaining wins are in making the model
   fill the fields, or in using a stronger generator.

## Threats to validity

- Synthetic sessions, one small local model, one sample per cell. Paired, not a
  significance test; the tie counts above show how thin several of the differences
  are.
- The generator and the measured model are the same 7B, so Results 1 and 2 are
  entangled: the minimal arm looks good partly because the sections it drops were
  empty anyway.
- The strong-model arm (deepseek-flash) saturated at 1.00 in v3, so it cannot
  resolve the minimal-vs-full question either. Doing that properly needs both a
  capable generator and a harder probe.
